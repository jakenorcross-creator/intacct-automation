FAQs

Accordion Panel Title 1 What is property and casualty insurance?
Content Spot 8
Property and casualty insurance protects individuals and businesses against non-life risks like property damage, liability, and certain specialty losses. It covers events such as auto accidents, fire, catastrophe, and worker injuries, with finance teams managing premium, claims, reinsurance, and compliance reporting.
Accordion Panel Title 2 What is the difference between property and casualty insurance?
Content Spot 9
Property insurance covers losses to physical assets like buildings or vehicles. Casualty insurance covers liability for injuries or damages to others. Many carriers underwrite both together, but finance must segment and report each line distinctly for statutory, GAAP, and management views.
Accordion Panel Title 3 What are the types of property and casualty insurance?
Content Spot 10
Property and casualty insurers write lines including auto, homeowners, commercial property, general liability, workers’ comp, professional liability, surety, and specialty risks. Each type drives unique reporting, claims, and capital requirements for finance teams.
Accordion Panel Title 4 How does Sage Intacct help with property and casualty accounting?
Content Spot 11
Sage Intacct automates multi-entity consolidation, dimensional reporting, and GAAP/statutory books—reducing manual reconciliations and close cycles. AP and allocations are streamlined, and regulatory reports are generated directly from your live data.
Accordion Panel Title 5 How does Sage Intacct support regulatory compliance?
Content Spot 12
Generate NAIC and state filings using configurable books, workflows, and automated audit trails. Maintain side-by-side GAAP, statutory, and tax reporting. Quickly adapt to new rules without heavy IT or spreadsheet work.
Accordion Panel Title 6 Why is multi-entity consolidation so painful for property and casualty insurance?
Content Spot 13
Insurers operate across multiple legal entities. Legacy finance stacks often require offline mapping and duplicate entries. Sage Intacct centralizes consolidation and manages eliminations, so you see combined ratios and surplus by program or state in seconds.
Accordion Panel Title 7 What is a property and casualty insurance license?
Content Spot 14
A property and casualty insurance license allows carriers and agents to legally underwrite and sell insurance in defined jurisdictions. Licensed entities must meet financial and regulatory standards—including strict accounting and reporting requirements tracked in Sage Intacct.
Accordion Panel Title 8 How does Sage Intacct integrate with core policy and claims systems?

Use open APIs and import tools to post financial data from policy, claims, and billing platforms directly into Sage Intacct. Integrate current and legacy systems for streamlined, audit-ready accounting—without brittle custom code.
Accordion Panel Title 9 How does Sage Intacct improve financial planning for insurers?
Content Spot 16
Move beyond spreadsheets. Automate rolling forecasts, driver-based planning, and what-if scenarios. Link actuals to plans, so your team responds quickly to CAT events, premium trends, or reserve reviews with data you trust.
Accordion Panel Title 10 What are the latest property and casualty insurance industry trends?
Content Spot 17
Premium growth is slowing and catastrophe risk is rising. Carriers must improve automation, reporting granularity, and scenario planning to adapt. Finance teams need to support AI, digital, and cloud initiatives without introducing new risks.
Accordion Panel Title 11 How does Sage Intacct help manage reinsurance complexity?
Content Spot 18
